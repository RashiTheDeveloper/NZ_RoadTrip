Must-knows for a first trip

Aotearoa is friendly and easy in cities, then suddenly alpine, tidal, or empty. These are the things that surprise people who have never driven here in December.

Drive on the left

Roundabouts give way to the right. One-lane bridges are common — read the small signs for who has priority. Do not treat 100 km/h as a promise; winding roads and campervans set the real pace.

Book the scarce tickets first

Before you fly: Interislander with the car (17 Dec), Waitomo, Hobbiton, a Rotorua hāngi evening, Kaikōura whale watch, a Milford cruise, and a Glenorchy LOTR tour. Parliament tours need an online booking and photo ID.

Hours, not kilometres on a screen

Auckland to Wellington is a full day. Queenstown to Milford is a very full day. Nelson to Kaikōura is about four hours. Kaikōura to Tekapo is about five. Build petrol stops in the Mackenzie and Fiordland.

Weather stacks in layers

Four seasons in a day is a cliché because it is true. Pack a rain shell for Milford even in midsummer. The alpine sun is fierce (thin ozone) — hat, SPF 50, sunglasses.

Emergency number is 111

Police, fire, ambulance. For non-urgent police, 105. Mountain and river accidents happen to fit people on “easy” walks. Tell someone your track and time.

Tides and geothermal ground

Cathedral Cove is happier near low-to-mid tide. Hot Water Beach pools work around low tide. Stay on marked paths at Wai-O-Tapu — the crust can break.

Sandflies and mosquitoes

Fiordland and many coasts have sandflies (namu). Repellent at dusk, especially Milford and Glenorchy. They are not a joke.

Biosecurity is serious

Clean boots and tent pegs between islands and parks. Do not move firewood. Declare food at the airport. Kauri dieback and pest-free islands depend on visitors doing the boring stuff.

Money and manners

GST is already in the price. Tipping is not required (rounding up for great service is fine). Cafés do excellent coffee. Shops may close on public holidays around Christmas — 25 Dec is shut.

Freedom camping is not free-for-all

Only certified self-contained vehicles in many zones, and never on random farmland. Fines are real. Use DOC camps and holiday parks in December.

Water looks inviting and kills

Rivers rise fast. Swim at patrolled beaches where you can. If a beach has no flags, treat rips as likely. Alpine tarns are colder than they look.

Earthquakes and tsunami blue lines

If the ground shakes strongly at the coast, move inland or uphill without waiting for a siren. Wellington and Kaikōura have marked tsunami routes.

Respect tapu and photography

Ask before photographing people, especially at a cultural performance or marae. Do not walk on graves, urupā, or closed tracks. Dual names (Aoraki / Mount Cook, Tāmaki Makaurau / Auckland) are not optional extras — they are the place.

Power sockets and cars

Type I sockets (same family as Australia). Rental excess, gravel roads, and unsealed lookouts can void insurance — read the contract. Keep left, indicate, and wave thanks on one-lane bridges.

Official updates: safetravel.govt.nz, metservice.com, and NZTA traffic for road closures.
