Before you go

Weather, dress code and timing

December is early summer: long days, mild lowlands, cold alpine nights, and weather that changes within an hour. Pack layers, not a heavy coat.

What to wear

Everyday

Light layers: T-shirts, a fleece and a light waterproof. Shorts are fine in the North Island by day. New Zealand is casual, and smart-casual is the dressiest you will need.

Evenings and culture

Rotorua, Wellington and the Redwoods walk get cool: bring a jacket. The Māori evening and Parliament tour call for neat, comfortable clothes and closed shoes.

Boats and fjords

Wind and spray on the Kaikōura whale watch, the ferry and the Milford cruise: windproof shell, beanie, non-slip shoes.

Walks

Hooker Valley is about 3 hours: sturdy shoes, a hat, water and a warm top. Weather turns fast at altitude.

Sun

NZ sun burns fast, even on cloudy days. Hat, sunglasses and SPF 50+, reapplied. Teenagers too.

Stargazing

Tekapo nights average about 7°C and feel colder when still. Thick jacket, beanie and gloves. Ask your tour if they lend warm coats.

Best time

Best time of year

Summer, December to February, gives the warmest and longest days, and is also the busiest and priciest. School holidays start in mid-December, so book beds and tickets early. Quieter, cooler months are October-November and March-April.

Best time of day

Hooker Valley early, before the crowds and winds. Whale watching on a morning sailing. Wellington and Queenstown lookouts near sunset, which is after 8:30 pm. Leave for Milford by 6 am.

Stargazing: pick the 19th

The moon is about 70% full on 19 Dec and about 80% on 20 Dec, with full moon on 24 Dec. A bright moon washes out the Milky Way, so the 19th is the darker night and the 20th is your backup if cloud cancels.

Eat and drink along the route

The Kai page covers the classics. Here is what to look for at each stop.

  • Auckland Fish and chips by the beach at Mission Bay, then a flat white.
  • Hobbiton The tour ends with a drink at the Green Dragon Inn.
  • Rotorua Hāngī at the Te Puia evening, steam-cooked in the ground.
  • Wellington Coffee capital and a craft beer city. Try both on the waterfront.
  • Picton and Nelson Marlborough green-lipped mussels and Sauvignon blanc; Nelson hops mean local craft beer and cider.
  • Kaikōura Crayfish: pricey, local, worth splitting. Kai means food, and here it means seafood.
  • Lake Tekapo Mackenzie high-country salmon and lamb.
  • Wānaka and Queenstown Central Otago pinot noir, a Cardrona Hotel pub lunch, and a Fergburger when the queue is short.

Tap water is safe to drink almost everywhere. The drink-drive limit is 0.05%, lower than in many countries, so choose a driver. The legal drinking age is 18 and bars ask for ID.

Before you fly

Entry rules

Many visitors need an NZeTA before flying, and Australian citizens do not. Check the requirements for your passport on the New Zealand Immigration website.

Insurance

Get travel insurance that covers medical care, cancelled ferry or helicopter flights, and rental-car excess.

Phone and money

Buy a local SIM or eSIM at the airport. Cards work almost everywhere, and tipping is optional.
